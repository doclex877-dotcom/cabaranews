Here is something that surprises many of the Zimbabweans, Nigerians, South Africans, and Kenyans I speak with: Australia is significantly more achievable as an immigration destination than its reputation suggests — and it is dramatically underutilised by African applicants compared to the UK and Canada.

The reasons are partly historical (Commonwealth ties drew Africans to the UK) and partly perceptual (Australia feels far and unknown). But the data tells a different story. Australia's points-tested immigration system is transparent, merit-based, and actively seeks skilled workers in a long list of occupations that map directly onto the professional backgrounds of many Africans seeking migration.

This guide explains how it works.

## The main skilled visa pathways

### Subclass 189 — Skilled Independent Visa

The most sought-after visa: permanent residence, no employer required, no state nomination required. You apply directly based on your points score and occupation.

**Requirements:**
- Occupation on the Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL)
- Skills assessment from the relevant Australian assessing authority
- Points score of at least 65 (competitive scores are typically 80–90+)
- English language requirement
- Age under 45

**Process:** Submit an Expression of Interest (EOI) through SkillSelect, receive an invitation based on your points score, then submit a full visa application.

### Subclass 190 — Skilled Nominated Visa

Permanent residence with a state or territory nomination. The nomination adds 5 points to your score and makes you competitive for a wider range of occupations and lower point thresholds.

Each Australian state and territory has its own skilled migration programme with specific occupation lists and requirements. Victoria, New South Wales, Queensland, South Australia, and Western Australia all run active programmes.

### Subclass 491 — Skilled Work Regional Visa

A 5-year provisional visa (not immediately permanent) for regional areas. Significantly lower points thresholds — some state streams have invited applicants with scores as low as 55. After 3 years of living and working in a regional area, you can apply for permanent residence (subclass 191).

This is an increasingly popular pathway, particularly for applicants whose points score is not competitive for a 189 or 190.

## The points test — what you actually need

Points are awarded for age, English, skilled employment, education, and other factors:

| Factor | Points available |
|---|---|
| Age 25–32 | 30 (maximum) |
| Superior English (IELTS 8+ or equivalent) | 20 |
| Competent English (IELTS 6+) | 0 (but required) |
| Proficient English (IELTS 7+) | 10 |
| Overseas skilled employment 8–10 years | 15 |
| Overseas skilled employment 5–8 years | 10 |
| Australian skilled employment 5–8 years | 15 |
| PhD from Australian institution | 20 |
| Bachelor's degree | 15 |
| State nomination (190) | 5 |
| Partner skills (nominated partner) | 5 |
| Community language | 5 |
| Study in regional Australia | 5 |
| Professional year in Australia | 5 |

**Competitive scores for 189 visas** have generally been 85–90+ for popular occupations in recent invitation rounds. Lower for state-nominated and regional visas.

## Step 1: Skills assessment

Before you can submit an EOI, your occupation and qualifications must be assessed by the relevant Australian assessing authority. Each profession has a designated body:

| Occupation | Assessing Authority |
|---|---|
| Engineers | Engineers Australia |
| Nurses | ANMAC |
| Accountants | CPA Australia / CAANZ / IPA |
| IT professionals | ACS (Australian Computer Society) |
| Teachers | AITSL |
| Doctors | AMC |
| Social workers | AASW |

A skills assessment verifies that your qualifications and experience meet Australian standards for your occupation. It typically takes 4–12 weeks and costs AUD $300–700 depending on the body.

**Important for African applicants:** Many assessing authorities require your qualifications to be verified, which may involve contacting your university directly or using a credential verification service. Start this process early — it is often the longest step.

## Step 2: English language test

All skilled visa applicants must demonstrate English proficiency. Accepted tests:

- **IELTS (Academic or General):** Competent English = 6.0 each band; Proficient = 7.0 each band; Superior = 8.0 each band
- **PTE Academic:** Equivalent score bands
- **TOEFL iBT:** Accepted with minimum scores specified by occupation

For most healthcare occupations, the OET (Occupational English Test) is accepted and may be more appropriate than IELTS.

The difference between Competent (IELTS 6.0) and Proficient (IELTS 7.0) is 10 points — potentially the difference between being invited and waiting indefinitely.

## Case study: Tafadzwa's pathway from Zimbabwe to Brisbane

Tafadzwa is a 33-year-old civil engineer from Harare who had been trying to move to the UK for three years. He had a Skilled Worker Visa but was unhappy with the UK's 2024 salary threshold increase, which made his employer reluctant to sponsor a new role.

A colleague mentioned the Australian points test. Tafadzwa calculated his score: age 33 (25 points), IELTS 7.5 (10 points), bachelor's degree in engineering (15 points), 8 years overseas experience (10 points). Total: 60 points. Not enough for a competitive 189 invitation.

He made three targeted improvements:

1. Retook IELTS and achieved 8.0 across all bands — gained 10 more points (Superior English). New total: 70.
2. Applied for Queensland state nomination (190) as a civil engineer — Queensland had civil engineering on their occupation list. Nomination awarded — plus 5 points. Total: 75.
3. Had his Engineering degree assessed by Engineers Australia — positive assessment received.

He submitted his EOI. Six months later he received an invitation for a Subclass 190 visa. His application was submitted, health checks and police clearances completed, and his visa granted 8 months after his EOI.

He moved to Brisbane with his wife and two children in early 2025. He had a job offer within 3 weeks of arriving.

## Occupations in demand — good news for African professionals

Australia's occupation shortage list is regularly updated. Consistently in demand in recent years:

- Nurses and midwives (virtually all specialties)
- Engineers (civil, mechanical, electrical, structural)
- IT professionals (software developers, cybersecurity, data analysts)
- Teachers (secondary school, special education, vocational)
- Accountants and auditors
- Social workers
- Construction project managers
- Medical imaging professionals

Many of these align directly with the professional backgrounds of educated Africans — particularly Zimbabweans, South Africans, Nigerians, Kenyans, and Ghanaians.

## State nomination strategies

If your points score is in the 65–80 range, state nomination is often the most effective strategy. Each state publishes an occupation list and has specific requirements:

**South Australia:** Historically one of the more accessible states for nomination, with a broad occupation list and a lower population threshold. Good option for applicants with 65–75 points.

**Western Australia:** Strong demand for mining, engineering, and trades-related occupations. Perth is a genuinely pleasant city with strong African diaspora communities.

**Tasmania:** Small population, actively seeks skilled migrants, lower points requirements. Less commonly considered but legitimately competitive.

**Victoria and NSW:** Most competitive — often require 80+ points and specific ties to the state.
